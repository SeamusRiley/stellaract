# What is a Stellaract?

A stellaract is a shape that combines a star and a square. It is often said in life that one cannot be both practical and idealistic—that one must be either an amoral pragmatist or an ill-fated tragic idealist. You’re either a starry-eyed dreamer with your head in the clouds, or a grounded, down-to-Earth square. Stars are one of the most popular artistic motifs because they represent a thing both beautiful and unattainable, and “star-chitect” is synonymous with impractical design. “Putting people into boxes” by contrast is synonymous with dehumanization, repression, and limitation. Well, not for me.

When I was designing my first buildings as a child my older brother used to peer over my shoulder and mockingly ask “Are you making BOXES?” and walk away laughing, and I would cheerfully answer “yes”, and not understand the derision of the comment. “What’s wrong with making boxes? I love boxes.” I loved drawing on graph paper. I loved wrapping Christmas presents in neat prisms. I loved my Rubik’s cubes. I loved neatly filling in data in Excel. As one can imagine, I was overjoyed when Minecraft came out.

The Stellaract vision is proudly monist. I do not believe in fragile “ideals” that work in theory, but crumble to pieces in practice. Neither do I entertain the idea that there is a boring, dutiful, paper-pushing, practical side to life and design that exists separate from a high, sparkling, beautiful, and unsustainable ideal. On the contrary, there is nothing less practical and less sustainable to me than to sacrifice beauty to the doldrums of expedience. I absolutely insist on being both a star and a square, and my designs have to be stellaract too. If a design is beautiful but doesn’t work well, or vice versa, into the trash can it goes. That is the stellaract way.

---

**stellaract** — *noun.* A shape that combines a star and a square.

**stellaract** — *adjective.* Integrating both facts and ideals.
